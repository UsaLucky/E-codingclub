1. What is AI?

Artificial Intelligence is a field of computer science dedicated to creating systems that perform tasks requiring human intelligence. Core capabilities: Learning, Reasoning, Problem Solving, Perception, Language Understanding. Two main categories: Narrow AI (task-specific) and AGI (Artificial General Intelligence).

2. Machine Learning

Machine Learning is a subset of AI where machines learn from data and improve without being explicitly programmed. Applications: spam detection, recommendation systems, CGPA prediction, medical diagnosis.

3. Brief History of AI

1950: AI idea conceived (Turing asked "Can machines think?"). 1956: AI officially born as a term. 1960–1980s: AI Winter — reduced funding. 1990s–2000s: IBM Deep Blue beats Kasparov; ML growth. 2010: Deep Learning era. 2012: Deep Neural Networks. 2017: Google Brain introduces Transformers. ChatGPT by OpenAI — Generative AI boom.

4. Generative AI & Tools

Generative AI creates new data (text, images, video, code). Main tools: GAN (Generator + Discriminator), VAE (Encoder + Decoder, latent space), Transformers (attention mechanism, LLMs), Diffusion Models (noise reversal). Applications: text creation, drug discovery, entertainment.

5. AI Agent & Smart Assistant

AI Agent: intelligent system that perceives environment, makes decisions, takes actions. Key parts: Perception, Cognition, Action & Goal. Smart Assistants: AI agents using natural language (e.g., Siri, Gemini, chatbots). Applications in healthcare, education, business, transportation.

6. Deep Learning, Neural Networks & LLMs

Deep Learning uses Artificial Neural Networks with multiple layers to analyse complex data. Neural Networks: fundamental architecture with interconnected nodes in layers. LLM (Large Language Model) — type of GenAI trained on massive text data. Key: Scale + Transformer Architecture. Uses: text generation, translation, summarisation, code generation.

7. AI Applications (NLP, Computer Vision, Robotics)

NLP: field enabling computers to understand human language. Apps: language translation, chatbots, sentiment analysis, speech recognition. Computer Vision: enables machines to see. Apps: face recognition, Google Lens, self-driving cars. Robotics: design, construction and operation of robots. Apps: logistics, healthcare co-bots, hazardous environments.
