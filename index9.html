COURSE NOTE: Internet Archive & the Wayback Machine

What it is · Why it matters for CSC 114

The Internet Archive (archive.org) is a non-profit digital library founded in 1996 by Brewster Kahle. It is one of the largest digital libraries in existence, storing over 700 billion web pages, 44 million books, 15 million audio recordings, and millions of software programs and videos — all freely accessible to anyone with an internet connection.

Its most famous feature is the Wayback Machine, a tool that lets you view snapshots of websites as they appeared at specific dates in the past. If a webpage is deleted or changed, the Wayback Machine preserves what it looked like — making it an essential tool for digital preservation, citation verification, and combating link rot.

Why it's relevant to this course: The Internet Archive is a real-world example of almost every concept in CSC 114 — it demonstrates digital preservation (LOCKSS strategy), metadata (Dublin Core), born-digital resources, open access, and the challenge of preserving the web as a cultural record. It also demonstrates the difference between a digital library (which it is) and a virtual library (which merely links to resources elsewhere).
